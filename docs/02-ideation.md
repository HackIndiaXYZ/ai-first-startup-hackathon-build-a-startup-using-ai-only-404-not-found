# 02 · Ideation: 25 fully mapped ideas

Generated by Claude (AI) from the research in `01-research.md`. Each idea is mapped across every dimension the hackathon judges: problem, payer, AI role, MVP, demo moment, data, business model, market, competition, moat, risks, and visual hook. Scores follow at the end of each card and in the summary table.

## Scoring rubric (100 points)

The rubric mirrors the official judging weights, translated into things that separate one idea from another. (AI-First Execution, the 25% criterion, depends on how we build, which is the same for every idea, so it shows up here as "AI depth in product" and "demo-ability".)

| Code | Dimension | Max | Maps to official criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | Problem severity, backed by verified 2026 data | 15 | Product & Innovation |
| O | Originality vs. the ~1,000-team field | 15 | Product & Innovation |
| A | AI depth in the product (agentic, multimodal, not a thin chat wrapper) | 15 | AI-First Execution, AI Usage & Workflow |
| D | Demo-ability: works for a stranger in 10 s, no keys, real data | 15 | Functionality & Execution |
| M | Market and business model: named payer, unit economics, scale | 15 | Market Potential |
| V | Visual / 3D storytelling potential (the user's brief + awwwards bar) | 10 | Demo & Pitch |
| B | Buildable to ship-ready quality before 1 Nov | 10 | Functionality & Execution |
| J | Judge resonance: India impact, "Most Scalable Idea" award fit | 5 | Market Potential, Demo & Pitch |

## Track: AI for Healthcare

### 1. ClaimBack: AI that fights rejected health-insurance claims
- **One-liner:** Upload the rejection letter and your policy; an agent finds the grounds, drafts the grievance, and walks you through insurer → Bima Bharosa → Ombudsman.
- **Problem:** ₹26,037 cr of health claims rejected or disallowed in FY24; 2.58 lakh grievances in FY25 (+20%); ~71% of Ombudsman complaints resolved for the policyholder. People lose winnable money because the process is opaque.
- **User / payer:** Policyholders (B2C success fee); hospitals' TPA desks (B2B); corporate HR (employee benefit).
- **AI in product:** Vision + long-document reasoning over 40-page policy wordings; clause matching ("waiting period", "pre-existing disease", "room-rent proportionate deduction"); grievance drafting; deadline tracking agent.
- **MVP:** Rejection-letter reader, clause finder, win-likelihood estimate, draft letters for 3 escalation levels, timeline tracker.
- **Wow moment:** The agent highlights the exact policy clause that contradicts the insurer's reason.
- **Data:** IRDAI Ombudsman rules, public policy wordings (insurer websites), IRDAI master circular on health (2024).
- **Business model:** 10 to 15% success fee on recovered amount; B2B ₹/case for hospitals.
- **Market:** ₹26k cr/yr disputed pool; 10% addressable → ₹2,600 cr; at 12% fee → ₹312 cr revenue pool.
- **Competitors:** Insurance Samadhan (human-led, funded), Claim Junction, lawyers.
- **Moat:** Clause-outcome dataset from Ombudsman awards (published).
- **Risks:** Needs a real LLM for long PDFs, weak offline demo; legal-advice liability; judges cannot verify outcomes live.
- **Visual hook:** Weak. Documents and timelines.
- **Score:** P 15 · O 11 · A 14 · D 8 · M 13 · V 3 · B 8 · J 5 = **77**

### 2. Generika: switch your prescription to Jan Aushadhi generics
- **One-liner:** Photograph a prescription; see the same molecule at a Jan Aushadhi Kendra for 50 to 90% less, and the nearest store.
- **Problem:** Medicines dominate out-of-pocket spend (~39% of health expenditure is OOP); 20,149 kendras exist but people don't know equivalents.
- **User / payer:** Patients (free); pharmacies (listing fee); insurers / employers (claims cost reduction).
- **AI in product:** Handwriting OCR of prescriptions, salt extraction, dose matching, interaction warnings.
- **MVP:** Prescription reader, salt → generic mapper (PMBI product list), savings calculator, store finder.
- **Wow moment:** "Your ₹2,340 monthly prescription costs ₹410 here."
- **Data:** PMBI product list (public), store locator data.
- **Business model:** Weak B2C; B2B with insurers and corporates.
- **Market:** Large impact, small revenue capture.
- **Competitors:** Government's own Jan Aushadhi Sugam app, 1mg / PharmEasy substitutes.
- **Moat:** Thin; government app is free.
- **Risks:** Handwriting OCR accuracy; medical liability; monetization.
- **Visual hook:** Low to medium (pill models).
- **Score:** P 13 · O 8 · A 10 · D 9 · M 7 · V 5 · B 8 · J 5 = **65**

### 16. Chhat: the independent AI solar advisor for every Indian roof
- **One-liner:** Read your electricity bill, model your roof in 3D with NASA sunlight data, size the system, stack every subsidy, audit the installer's quote, and after installation, audit the DISCOM bill.
- **Problem:** ~49 lakh homes still to go solar under PM Surya Ghar; CEEW finds only 5% of households willing to install even where economics work. Documented pain: overpriced or non-compliant quotes (ALMM List-II since Jun 2026), wrong DISCOM bills after install (MNRE, Jul 2026), net meters not activated, consumer-court cases over missed savings.
- **User / payer:** Homeowners (free); installers pay for verified leads (₹1,500/lead + 2% on conversion); banks/NBFCs pay loan-origination fees; installer SaaS (₹2,999/month); DISCOM / state agencies (B2G dashboards).
- **AI in product:** Agentic advisor with tools (bill reader via vision, irradiance lookup, sizing, subsidy, finance, quote audit, bill audit), Hindi/English conversation; deterministic engine as fallback.
- **MVP:** Roof Report (3D), Quote Check, Bill Audit, Ask Chhat agent, installer landing.
- **Wow moment:** The 3D roof fills with panels as the sun sweeps across a Delhi afternoon, then the quote audit flags ₹38,000 of overpricing and a non-DCR panel that would forfeit the ₹78,000 subsidy.
- **Data:** NASA POWER irradiance (live, CORS-enabled), MNRE subsidy rules, CEA emission factor, user's own bill.
- **Business model:** Marketplace take rate ~₹6,000 to 8,000 per converted home, SaaS, loan fees.
- **Market:** 49 lakh remaining scheme homes × ~₹1.65 lakh ≈ ₹80,000 cr installation value in the scheme window alone; 32 GW economic residential potential (CEEW).
- **Competitors:** Installers with calculators (SolarSquare, Freyr, Loom), which carry a conflict of interest; government portal (process, not advice). No independent quote/bill auditor found.
- **Moat:** Independence + the post-install bill-audit dataset (who bills wrong, where) + installer quality scores from audited quotes.
- **Risks:** Subsidy window ends Mar 2027 (mitigation: economics hold without subsidy; C&I and batteries next); data accuracy (mitigation: cite every number, user can edit).
- **Visual hook:** Exceptional. A 3D Indian rooftop (water tank, parapet, clothesline) with a real sun path.
- **Score:** P 14 · O 14 · A 12 · D 15 · M 14 · V 10 · B 9 · J 5 = **93**
